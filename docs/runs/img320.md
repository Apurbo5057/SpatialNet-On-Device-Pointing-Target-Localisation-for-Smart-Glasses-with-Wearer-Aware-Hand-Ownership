# Run: 320 px input (`runs/img320/`)

## Setup

- **Kaggle kernel:** `spatialnet-train-320`.
- **New private dataset:** `egopoint-spatialnet-320`.
  - Re-converted from the original images at 320×320; not the 224 images upsampled.
  - Every label file is identical to the 224 dataset (checked file by file), so only image detail differs.
- **Same as the main run:** code, model (722,104 parameters), settings and seed 42.
- **Flag:** `--img_size 320`.
- **Model at 320 px:** grid 40×40, compute 0.92 GFLOPs (4× the 160 px model).

## Training health

- No errors in the log.
- Early stopping after 84 epochs, best checkpoint at epoch 59: the same stopping point as the 224 run. 73 s per epoch, 1 h 43 min.
- **Validation loss is higher than at 160 px (0.442 vs 0.406), yet the metrics are better.** The heatmap losses sum over more grid cells at higher resolution, so loss values are not comparable across resolutions. A side effect: early stopping on that loss may stop high-resolution runs too soon, so the 224 and 320 runs are probably under-trained.

## Results: the resolution trend

Same test images; `*` marks a 95% interval of the difference from the previous resolution that excludes 0.

| Metric | 160 px | 224 px | 320 px | 224 → 320 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Target P@0.5 | 58.9 | 62.4 | **65.1** | +2.7 * |
| Target P@0.7 | 31.8 | 37.1 | **40.3** | +3.2 * |
| Target mIoU | 53.3 | 54.8 | **56.5** | +1.8 * |
| On-object P@0.5 | 66.0 | 68.3 | **71.2** | +2.8 * |
| Near P@0.5 | 49.2 | 53.8 | **57.4** | +3.7 |
| **Far P@0.5** | 25.4 | **35.9** | 34.9 | −1.0 (flat) |
| Small targets (<3% of image), P@0.5 | 36.9 | 33.0 | **46.6** | +13.6 |
| Hand mIoU | 73.7 | 74.9 | **76.4** | +1.5 * |
| Fingertip error (% of width) | 2.43 | 1.81 | **1.32** | −0.49 * |
| Direction error (degrees) | 32.2 | 31.4 | 31.6 | flat |
| Target P@0.5 with other hands | 60.1 | 63.9 | **66.1** | +2.2 * |
| GFLOPs | 0.23 | 0.45 | 0.92 | — |
| CPU latency, 1 thread, PyTorch (ms) | 25.7 | 37.2 | 50.8 | — |

## Interpretation

- **Resolution keeps helping overall.** From 160 to 320 px: P@0.5 +6.2, P@0.7 +8.5, mIoU +3.2, all with the same 0.72M parameters.
- **Returns per doubling of compute are steady but modest:** +3.5 P@0.5 for 160 → 224, then +2.7 for 224 → 320.
- **Far targets saturate at 224 px** (about 35%). The direction error does not improve at any resolution (about 31.5°). Far targets are limited by the direction estimate, not by the input.
- **Small targets need real detail.** They gain only at 320 px (36.9 → 46.6), where the re-converted images carry detail the 224 images lack.
  - The paper currently says "small objects do not benefit; the size head is the limit". **That is now contradicted and must be revised.**
  - The size head still over-predicts small boxes (×1.6), but less than before (×1.9).
- **The hand and fingertip keep improving:** fingertip error halves from 2.43% to 1.32%.
- **Behaviour with other hands is unaffected or better:** P@0.5 with pasted hands 66.1, no-hand frames kept 94.8.

## Cost and choice of headline model

| Input | P@0.5 | Far P@0.5 | GFLOPs | CPU ms (rough) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 160 | 58.9 | 25.4 | 0.23 | 26 |
| 224 | 62.4 | 35.9 | 0.45 | 37 |
| 320 | 65.1 | 34.9 | 0.92 | 51 |

- **224 px is the best trade-off:** it gets nearly all of the far-target gain at half the compute of 320.
- **320 px is best in absolute terms**, if the glasses can afford about 1 GFLOP per frame. This depends on the T2 measurements.
- **All three have the same 0.72M parameters**, so the "tiny model" claim holds for each.

## Caveats

- One seed per resolution.
- The 224 and 320 runs stopped early (best epoch 59), so they are probably under-trained, which may understate the gain.
- The 320 dataset re-encodes the images, a small extra difference besides resolution.
